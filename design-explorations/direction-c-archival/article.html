Student senate approves widened library hours, citing exam-week demand

After two readings and a sustained debate over staffing costs, the measure passes on a twelve-to-three vote. The library will remain open until 2 a.m. during final weeks, effective spring term.

Whitman, Robert E. · Staff Writer · 1,240 words · Section: Campus News

Cite this article Download PDF View page scan Permalink

The Student Senate on Tuesday night approved, by a vote of twelve to three, a proposal to extend library hours during final examination weeks, ending a two-month deliberation that had occupied four of the body's last six meetings. Under the measure, the Slocum Library will remain open until 2 a.m. each night of the designated exam period, beginning with the spring term in May.

The debate, which stretched past 11:15 p.m. in Merrick Hall, turned less on the merits of extended hours than on the question of who would staff them. Proponents led by Vice President Margaret Deane '61 cited a petition signed by 683 students — roughly forty percent of the undergraduate body — calling for the change. Opponents, including Treasurer James Overbeek '60, pressed for cost estimates they said had not been provided.

"We have asked the library for these numbers twice," Mr. Overbeek said during the final exchange, "and twice we have been told to vote on principle."

The vote in context

The extended-hours proposal joins a broader pattern of senate-driven reforms this academic year. Earlier measures have included revising the academic honor code, creating a standing committee on residence-hall dining, and — at the body's October meeting — the establishment of a formal appeals process for academic probation.

President Ann Whitlow '60, presiding, told senators after the vote that the measure would take effect for the May exam period "provided the library and the Office of Student Affairs can jointly reach agreement on staffing." She said she expected discussions to conclude by the March meeting.

It is a small thing, but small things accumulate. A library open at one in the morning is a different library than one that closed at ten. Feltcher, H. A. — Department of History. Quoted in Whitman 1960, p. 1.

Staffing and cost

Under the amended proposal, two staff members will be on duty during the extended hours: one professional librarian and one student assistant. The senate's Finance Committee estimated the additional labor cost at approximately $340 per exam week — a figure Mr. Overbeek disputed as "incomplete on its face."

The Finance Committee noted that its estimates assumed no change in the current utility contract. Any renegotiation, the committee wrote, could alter the figure "materially either upward or downward."

Reaction

Student reaction in the hours following the vote was largely favorable. A short survey conducted in the dining halls found 84 percent of respondents "pleased" or "strongly pleased" with the outcome. A smaller fraction, nine percent, said they were "neutral"; four percent described the change as "unnecessary."

In the Sanders-Lowry dormitory lounge, where this reporter observed the reactions of several residents, the most common response was surprise that the proposal had required the full two months.

What the amendment does

  • Extends Slocum Library closing time from 10 p.m. to 2 a.m. during final exam weeks.
  • Applies to the fall (December) and spring (May) exam periods.
  • Requires joint approval of staffing by the library and Student Affairs.
  • Sunsets after two years pending senate review.

What it does not

  1. Extend hours outside of exam weeks.
  2. Add weekend evening hours beyond the current closing time of 10 p.m. on Sundays.
  3. Modify quiet-study rules, which remain in force after 10 p.m. during all extended-hour operation.

A transcript of the meeting will be available in the senate office by Friday.
