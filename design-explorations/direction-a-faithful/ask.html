You asked

How did The Transcript cover the library hours debate in early 1960?

The archive answers

The library hours debate appears in at least four issues of The Transcript between November 1959 and January 1960, with the most detailed coverage in the January 13 front-page story by Robert E. Whitman1. The issue was framed from the outset less as a question of merit than of cost and staffing, which both supporters and opponents returned to repeatedly.

In the November 4 issue, the senate's initial proposal was introduced during a brief report in the body's weekly meeting; at that time, no vote was taken, and the matter was sent to the Finance Committee for study2. A follow-up report in the December 9 issue noted that the committee had "requested but not received" a staffing estimate from the library administration3.

Caveat · low confidence

Only two of the four issues referenced above are fully indexed in the archive as of this query; the November 25 Thanksgiving-week issue is missing pages 2–4, and some context about the original motion's author may be absent.

The January 13 vote was twelve-to-three in favor. Supporters cited a petition of 683 students1; opponents, led by Treasurer James Overbeek, pressed unsuccessfully for cost figures13.
