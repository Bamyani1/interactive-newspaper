## Oberlin Experimental Plan: Grades Become Optional

*By Don Bumpus*

Oberlin College students may usher in 1970 by bidding farewell to the traditional A-B-C-D-F grading system.

A one semester experiment allows any student to receive ungraded credit for a full schedule of course work ordinarily evaluated at D level or higher, according to the student newspaper, The Oberlin Review.

The new "credit/no entry" option includes courses in a student's major field.

From Oberlin's previous system, students retain the option of taking only one course per semester on a satisfactory/unsatisfactory (S/U) basis. Work at C- level or better is labeled satisfactory. This option does not include major field courses.

According to the Review, the Grading Committee which developed the program said the previous system "inhibits the student's motivation to discuss his work with the professor and other students and to seek a more complete evaluation of his work." The experiment tests the validity of this contention, the Review said.

The committee, after discussing criteria and methods, will evaluate the experiment in the spring, according to the Review.

OWU juniors and seniors may opt for pass/fail evaluation of one course per term. The course must be numbered over 50 and may not be in the major field of study. Individual professors determine the pass level for each course.

Discussion concerning revisions of OWU's grading system has begun in the Academic Policy committee, according to Robert Lisensky, vice president for academic affairs.

A new program allowing freshman English failures to repeat the freshman course is being developed, according to Lisensky. The F received in the first course will not enter the student's accumulative point average, Lisensky said.

## Journalism Grant

OWU's Journalism Department has received a third $1,000 grant from the Reader's Digest Foundation for maintenance of a Reader's Digest Research-Travel Fund.

Money from this fund is used to help students carry out research for journalism class projects and to cover special stories for The Transcript.

Money from the fund was used to cover the expenses of five Transcript staff members who reported the Nov. 15 Vietnam Moratorium in Washington, D.C.
