## Fraternity, Sorority Rush Begins; Attendance Down

OWU's twelve fraternities and ten sororities began rush this week with 222 freshman men and about 320 women signed up as of last Sunday night. Last year 275 men and about 380 women participated in rush.

The drop in attendance is attributed to a smaller Freshman Class.

This year's rush differs from past years because Chi Phi fraternity, which dropped out of IFC last spring, is holding rush sessions at different times than scheduled sessions in the IFC houses.

According to Bill Watson, IFC rush chairman, there will be one rush session tonight with two sessions both Thursday and Friday nights. The first session begins at 6:30 p.m. and the second at 8:30. Tonight's single session is from 7-9 p.m.

Watson said an open session will be held Saturday from 1-3 p.m. Any rushee may then visit any house he desires.

Pledging will take place Saturday 4-8 p.m. and Sunday from 1 p.m. on.

According to Watson, in order to pledge a freshman must have a 2.00 accum, but those with accums between 1.5 and 2.00 may be honorary pledges until they reach the 2.00 requirement. An honorary pledge can not accept a pledge pin or officially sign fraternity forms until he satisfies the grade requirement.

Sorority rush began yesterday with four 45-minute open houses which continue tonight and tomorrow. According to junior Cindy Jones, Kappa Kappa Gamma rush chairman, there will be one hour informal parties this Saturday and Sunday.

## Spencer Receives Essay Honor

Top honors in American literature have been presented to Ben Spencer, former OWU professor of English.

Spencer received the Norman Foerster Award for the best essay published last year in "American Literature," the "finest and most important journal in American literature," according to Samuel M. Pratt, chairman, Department of English.

The American literature section of the Modern Languages Association, publishers of "American Literature," presented the award. A $250 cash prize was included.

Spencer said his winning essay, "Sherwood Anderson: Mythopoeiest," was published in March. He said the work is a study of Anderson's work in terms of his attitude and general treatment of the American scene.

## 'Sensitivity' Set

The first of eight encounter labs planned for this term's sensitivity training program will be held Friday.

Interested students should sign up for the 24-hour sessions in the Religious Life Office, Phillips 52, according to University Chaplain James S. Leslie.
