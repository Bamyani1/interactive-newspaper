Riverside accreditation decision due

The Riverside School of Nursing is expecting to hear the National League of Nursing's (NLN) decision on OWU's accreditation sometime "this week," according to Carol Karrer, assistant professor of nursing.

The 18-member Board of Review is meeting this week in New York City to review OWU's 625 page application and "some additional information" supplied by site visitors, according to Fay Carol Reed, dean of nursing.

Reed said OWU will be notified May 1 by letter of the Board's decision.

Audrey Conbley of the University of Southern Maine and Helen Johnson of Purdue University visited the school Feb. 12-15, Karrer said. The two women are deans of their respective schools.

The two spoke with students, faculty and administrators and took their analysis to the NLN. The Board will review the self-study report, which describes the services of the program, and organization, faculty, curriculum, resources and evaluate it according to its criteria.

Reed estimated "10,000 man hours" were spent writing the application. Karrer said plans for the application were begun when the school of nursing was formed in July, 1975.

Reed said one-third of schools that apply for accreditation the first time do not receive it.

Reed said accreditation would "give students an added advantage in the job market" and Karrer said it would have "a positive effect" on enrollment.

If the school of nursing receives accreditation, it will be listed as an NLN accredited institution for four to eight years. Should the application be denied, the school will receive a list of shortcomings and will have the option of correcting them and reapplying, Karrer said.

School replacement finalists set; candidate interviews begin today

John L. Lowe will be on campus today and tomorrow as the first of five finalists to interview for the position of dean of educational services, according to Provost Kenneth Goodrich.

Lowe is director of student development services at Frostburg State College in Frostburg, Md.

Lowe will meet with Goodrich; Lauren Wilson, dean of academic affairs; Robert Meyer, vice president for business affairs; the Counseling, Advising and Placement (CAP) Center personnel and other administrators, faculty and staff.

He will meet students tonight at dinner in Smith Hall with junior Leanne Walker and sophomore Brian Ward, student members of the Search Committee.

Friday, he will meet with the Student Life Office staff, University President Thomas Wenzlau and the Search Committee, among others, Goodrich said.

Charlie Nelms, associate dean for academic affairs and dean of the University Division at Indiana University Northwest in Gary, will visit on Monday and Tuesday. Judith M. Gappa, project director and director of affirmative action --equal opportunity programs at Utah State University in Logan, will be here next Wednesday and Thursday.

Susan I. Bolman, vice president for student services at the New York Institute of Technology in Old Westbury will visit April 28-29, while Donald H. Craig, assistant vice president for student services at the State University of New York in Oswego, will come April 30-May 1.

The five were selected from 16 semi-finalists chosen before spring break on March 21. One hundred and five applications were received for the position.

Goodrich said the committee will be two to three weeks behind its original deadline of April 15 for choosing a replacement for Stephen Scholl.

Scholl resigned in November to become dean of general studies at John F. Kennedy University in Orinda, Calif.
