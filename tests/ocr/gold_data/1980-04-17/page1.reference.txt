District rep. assumes TKE chapter control

Dennis Perry, Tau Kappa Epsilon (TKE) district representative, said he has assumed "absolute authority and responsibility" for the OWU TKE chapter.

Perry, reached at TKE national headquarters in Indianapolis, Ind., said he "asked for and received the resignations" of sophomores Jon Hummel, chapter president; Scott Robertson, vice-president and Jason Luckenbach, secretary, April 6.

Perry is at TKE to help "educate the members" in the organizational aspects of a fraternity, according to Gary Rybak, coordinator of Greek affairs. Rybak said the only way for Perry to achieve this is by maintaining "total control."

TKE has been on probation by its national chapter for almost a year.

None of the former TKE officers would comment on their resignations, but one TKE member, who wished to remain anonymous, said, "Perry came in here, told us, 'I am law,' and told the officers that they were resigning."

The member said Perry had "bad vibes when he first came to the (TKE) house, but after a couple days, he realized we weren't mass murderers."

Perry said a district representative's removal of officers is "standard procedure."

Perry said he expected to return to Delaware yesterday. He said during the four weeks he intends to remain at TKE, he will reorganize and restructure the house. He added, "the resignations are the beginning of the rebuilding process."

Perry said he would maintain control of all TKE rush activities and social activities.

According to figures released by the registrar, TKE had the highest grade point average among fraternities winter term (2.78). Rybak said this would be good for TKE, but he added "the organizational aspect is as important as the scholastic aspect".

1980-81 aid to cover tuition, board hike

Financial aid is available to cover next year's $820 fee increase, according to Edward Irish, director of financial aid.

Many of next year's freshmen have already received their financial aid packages, according to Lynne Mabus, assistant director of financial aid. She said upperclassmen should get their packages by the end of spring term.

Since the financial aid staff is still receiving aid applications, it is unsure how many students will apply. Last year, 721 students received aid.

Mabus said, "I envision the percentage of students on aid going up considerably next year." She attributed this to families' increased awareness of aid's availability as well as the fee increase.

OWU offers about $2.2 million in financial aid each year, including funds allocated by the government. This money is divided among grants, work study and University loans.

Irish said loans guaranteed by the government but arranged through private banks probably amount to another $1.1 million.

Irish said grants make up the largest percentage of aid offered because most students receive half their need in grant. The remaining half is divided between work-study and loans.

This year, for the first time, different work-study jobs received different hourly wages. At the beginning of the school year, students with a work-study job could earn from $2.90-$3.50 an hour. When minimum wage increased in January, student wages also increased. Students can now earn up to $3.70 an hour. Last year, all workers received $2.65.

Irish said these pay differences were based on faculty and administrative job evaluations. He said the different pay levels are working out well and will be continued.

OWU to pay part of bill for new Rickey roof

The administration is going ahead with plans for roof repairs on Branch Rickey Center and Gray Chapel, despite the lack of a settlement in either case, according to Robert Meyer, vice-president for business affairs.

"Probably only half" of a new Branch Rickey Center roof will be paid for by Johns-Mansville, manufacturer of the roof shingles, according to Meyer.

Meyer said it will cost between $135,000 to $150,000 to re-roof Branch Rickey, but the settlement suggested by Johns-Mansville offers only $71,000.

"We are hoping to recover an additional $15,000 in expenses," Meyer said. Negotiations with the manufacturer are being handled by George Bodwell, chairman of the Facilities Committee of the Board of Trustees.

Meyer said the University is waiting for Bodwell's report before deciding whether to accept the settlement.

The original roof cost $90,000 five years ago, Meyer said. "We've had to absorb $14,000 to $15,000 worth of repairs since Branch Rickey Center was built," he added.

Meyer said the roof began leaking two years after its completion. "We've had as many as 40 cans catching water at one time," he added.

The shingle company is responsible for the Branch Rickey roof problem because the shingles are defective, Meyer said. "You can actually see them cracking — they're brittle," he added.

Roof problems also plague Gray Chapel's roof, Meyer said. Some of these problems are due to shingles, but there is no connection between the two roof problems, Meyer said.

Applications up 1%, class of 685 projected

Admissions applications for next year are up slightly more than one percent according to figures released by Peter Feickert, director of admissions.

However, the number is down nearly seven percent from 1978, when 2,241 applications had been received by this time.

Feickert said admissions received 2,064 applications as of April 13, 1979 and had received 2,089 as of April 11, 1980. OWU has admitted 1,583 applicants and received 131 deposits. Last year at this time, 1,774 students were accepted and 147 deposits received.

Feickert said accepted students have until May 1 to notify OWU of their decision, but that some have been granted deferrals in order to hear from other schools and financial aid offices.

He plans to open the "wait list" around May 6 and hopes to "top off" admissions with 15 to 20 students from that list.

The projected class size for next year is 685. Last year's class was 746, Feickert said.

Feickert said he will continue to admit qualified candidates throughout the summer session. "We will stop at whatever point we think the freshman class is locked up," he said.

The Admissions Office is sponsoring "A Slice of College Life" program this weekend for 316 accepted students in a final recruiting effort.
