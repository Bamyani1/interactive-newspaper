Batmen lose to Otters

The baseball team has an 0-4 record in the Ohio Athletic Conference (OAC), after losing to Otterbein 6-5 Friday at Westerville.

Pitching has been the main problem so far. The pitching staff's earned run average is 8.26.

"Good offensive play and the rapid improvement of (senior) Steve Anderson has kept us in all the games we've played this year," coach Les Michael said.

The team has been impressive offensively, with a batting average of .302. Senior Ken Boulter leads the team at .471, followed by Anderson, at .462.

Against Otterbein, the Bishops got off to a 5-0 lead after two innings. The team scored four runs in the first inning on three hits, a walk and a sacrifice.

In the second inning, senior Jeff Barhoff started a two-out rally with a base hit up the middle. Barhoff then scored on successive base hits by Anderson and Boulter.

Sophomore Pete Germano allowed just one run on five hits through the first five innings. But in the sixth inning, Otterbein scored four runs, tying the score 5-5.

Otterbein scored the winning run in the bottom of the eighth inning on two base hits and a sacrifice.

Just one first for tracksters

The women's track team traveled to Bowling Green State University to compete in the Bowling Green Invitational Saturday.

The competition was mainly AIAW Division I and II schools. While the team scored only 10 points, it did beat Wittenberg and Defiance, Division III schools. Central Michigan won the meet.

Sophomore sprinter and co-captain Sabrina Spencer won the 400-meter dash with a time of 59.9. She was the only OWU runner to place in the meet.

Runners fourth in Relays

The weather was damp, overcast and drizzly for Saturday's Otterbein Relays featuring the Ohio Athletic Conference's six best track and field teams, and the weather reflected OWU's dismal performance.

After capturing the Great Lakes Colleges Association title a week earlier, the Bishops found the competition a lot tougher at Otterbein, placing fourth out of six teams.

Baldwin-Wallace (B-W) won seven of eight running events en route to winning the Relays. The Yellow Jackets piled up 112 points to top Mt. Union (84), Otterbein (76), OWU (60), Ohio Northern (50) and Wittenberg (36).

OWU's thinclads failed to win a single event. The shuttle hurdle relay team of seniors Dennis Hearns and Paul Curry and junior Greg Bollenbacher finished second (46.0), four-tenths of a second behind B-W.

The sprint medley relay team of freshman Cory Glenn, sophomore Steve Rohner, junior Kelvin Johnson and senior Darold Johnson finished second in 3:35.9. The only other second place finish belonged to the 400-meter relay team of freshman Doug Ansary, Bollenbacher and the Johnson duo in 43.7.

The team's performance in the field events was noticeably hurt by the absence of senior Brian Baker, who quit the team last week. Baker, co-holder of the OWU high jump record at 6'8" with Hearns, also played a vital role in the long jump and triple jump.

While coach Marv Frye said he was not overly disappointed in the team's performance, he said certain areas were a problem. "Our distance team didn't do as well as I hoped and without Baker our field events didn't do much."

Frye added that last week's rainy weather hindered practice on Selby Stadium's cinder track. "Hopefully, we can close the gap on our competition with some better weather."

Saturday the thinclads travel to Wooster for a meet with the Scots and Mt. Union.

Frye said he is considering sending a 1600-meter intermediate hurdles relay team to the Akron Relays, also held Saturday.

Spikers third; Taylor is MVP

When was the last time you heard of a team finishing the season with a game record of 103-29? Been a while? Look no further than the men's volleyball team, which ended its season with that record and tied for third place in the Midwest Intercollegiate Volleyball Association (MIVA).

The team's third place ranking came after the MIVA tournament April 12 at the University of Michigan. OWU split all of its games, playing Calvin College, Illinois, Bowling Green and Notre Dame.

The 4-4 record gave the Bishops an overall fifth place finish in the tournament and second in their twelve team division. Miami won the tournament.

The tournament highlight for the Bishops was when senior Dave Taylor was chosen the tournament's Most Valuable Player and was voted to the All-tournament first team. "I'm going to cry when Dave graduates," coach Bill Reed said. "He is unquestionably the best player I have ever coached or seen."

Reed said Taylor's play at the championship, as well as through the year, was so outstanding, the balloting for MVP was not even close.

Sophomore Scott Smyth was chosen to the All-tournament second team.

Despite its success, the team had hoped to do better, as they beat Miami consistently in regular season play. "We did not play as well as would have liked," Taylor said. "Our bumping was not as good as it should have been, and our sets and hits suffered because of it."

Reed said the team should also do well next year, as only two players are graduating. Along with Taylor, Dave Hazard, who Reed said is "a consistently excellent performer," is graduating.

Next year's squad will be led by Smyth, who Reed said "will be awesome." The team will be balanced by returning freshmen Scott Hays and Scott Muir, as well as the team's back row specialist, sophomore Ben Levey.

Stickwomen hurt by loss of star goalie

If the Philadelphia 76ers lost Dr. J, or if the Pittsburgh Steelers lost Terry Bradshaw, both coaches would have a hard time finding a replacement.

Mary Parker, coach of the women's lacrosse team, found herself in that position since All-Midwest goalie Mary McCracken graduated last year.

McCracken, the team's only senior last year, was OWU's star player, according to Parker. "It's hard to replace someone at such a key position," she said.

Senior Lori Murphy has the job of replacing McCracken, and with the present young team in front of her, it is not going to be easy.

Last year's inexperienced team had a 5-4 record in the Midwest Women's Lacrosse (MWWL) district.

One of the eight returnees for the Bishops is senior Cathy Nolan, who Parker considers one of the best cover points in the MWWL. Senior Pam Moore was the team's leading scorer last year, and made the All-Midwest second team along with sophomore Katie Shelley.

Despite seven goals by Moore, averaging three goals per game, the Bishops lost at Bowling Green Tuesday 11-10.

Saturday the Bishops traveled to Richmond, Ind. and lost to Ball State 10-6 and to Earlham 12-4.

LaX team crushes Denison; rematch only tough game left

Coach Jay Martin said before the season that Ohio State and Denison would give the men's lacrosse team its toughest games in the Midwest. That may hold true yet, but neither team has presented much of a problem in the initial matchup.

OWU beat the Buckeyes 14-7 on April 2, then exploded for 12 second-half goals to beat Denison, ranked eighth in National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA) Division III, 15-6 Saturday at Roy Rike Field.

Now comes the easy part. Having beaten their toughest competition on the regular season schedule, only a few weak teams and a Denison rematch (May 3) stand between the Bishops and the playoffs.

How does a coach keep the players interested? After all, Wooster (whom OWU played yesterday) lost to Denison 15-1.

"When we play a weaker team like Wooster, we try to keep them out of the picture and concentrate on team objectives, such as scoring four goals per quarter or only letting in two a game," Martin said. "The players know they have to play hard in order to stay in the starting lineup. That should be enough incentive."

Both the players and Martin agree in the last few games, the team has shown better teamwork, an improvement which has led to a composite score of 50-22 over their Midwest opponents.

"We're seeing a lot more people passing up a possible goal to pass for a sure goal," Martin said. "Unselfish play like that really gives the rest of the team a boost."

Perhaps the best indication of the Bishops' improvement was their play in the second half on a wet field Saturday. Junior Denison goalie Bruce Pailet was stopping OWU on all low shots. The Bishops went to the good percentage high shot, which the Big Red had trouble handling.

Junior Don Sheehan led the attack with six goals, while senior Kevin Nugent had four. Senior Mark McCarthy and junior Mark Goldsmith each had a goal and four assists.

"I looked at the game as a good even matchup," Denison coach Tommy Thompson said. "But they took advantage of our breakdowns and executed very well. They're as good as any team we've seen this year."

What did the lopsided victory mean for the Bishops?

"It means we can go back to Denison for a rematch knowing we're the better team," Martin said. "If we fall behind two goals, we'll know we can come back."
