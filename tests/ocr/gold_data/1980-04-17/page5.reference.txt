Soph. says letter 'completely failed'

Editor, The Transcript,

After reading Stephen Beck's letter in the Transcript (April 10), which was written in criticism of an article by David Byrd, entitled "Black s snubbed," I was left with the feeling that he and I had read entirely different articles.

In a vain attempt to "read between the lines" of "Blacks snubbed," Beck completely failed in his analysis of it. He accused Byrd of ignoring what he describes as the complementary give and take that occurs between black and white society. Historical evidence will show that when white society "takes" from black and other non-white societies, it often fails to credit the source. It's as if white society doesn't want to admit that non-whites have made important contributions to world civilizations.

Hence, we have whites who have fooled themselves into believing that Bo Derek invented "cornrows" or Christopher Columbus discovered America when the people he called "Indians" had been living here for at least 25,000 years before his "discovery."

The first paragraph in Byrd's article further illustrates the point. The fact that the United States officially recognizes the accomplishments of blacks only during Feb., Black History Month, is an attempt to belittle those accomplishments. No respectable historian would consider limiting the time one could learn about Thomas Edison or Eli Whitney. But such is the case when blacks like Charles R. Drew (established first experiment in production of blood plasma and founded the first blood banks) and Garrett A. Morgan (inventor of the gas mask) are only heard about during Black History Month and rarely ever in traditional (white) history courses.

Beck seems to be under the illusion that Byrd's separation of society into black and white is artificial. I really can't see how any American can make such a statement. In any multi-racial U.S. community, there can be found a park, street, river, railroad track - as is the case here in Delaware - or any other object which serves as a barrier to separate the races. In such communities, there also can be found schools (unless they are integrated by force), swimming pools, parks, dance halls, bars and many other establishments which are recognized as being either for whites or non-whites.

And on Sunday morning, the racial separation is most visible when Americans go to church.

Finally, Beck concludes Byrd's final thesis to be that blacks are still snubbed by whites despite their friendly and reasonable nature. In truth, Byrd only said that black students were more likely to attend programs by whites while whites can rarely be seen at programs by blacks unless of course a big name like Dick Gregory or Kwame Toure (formerly Stokely Carmichael) is the attraction.

This also holds true for the nations as a whole. Whatever is good for whites is good for all, but whatever is good for non-whites is only good for non-whites. Example: a white woman can represent all women in the U.S. by becoming Miss America while a black woman can only represent black women in the U.S. as Miss Black America.

The only point I'm in agreement with Beck is the one in which he contends that anyone who truly seeks to alleviate racial tension would do well to consult such sources (reasoned publications on the discussion of racial matters)." I suggest to Beck that the next time he writes a letter on such matters that he follows his own advice.

Alcohol policy queried

Editor, The Transcript,

What is the purpose of this new alcohol policy?

Is it to prevent students from getting drunk and ripping up the campus? I hope not. I think if I were fined for drinking a beer while standing on the porch of my fraternity house, I would be tempted to wait until I was stone sober and methodically earn the irrational fine with an equally irrational act.

Is it to lend an air of seriousness to University leaders' recent attempts to improve OWU's academic reputation? I hope not. One has only to check the credentials of each year's incoming freshman class to see that an alcohol policy, any alcohol policy, is a sadly misdirected effort.

Is it to bring Ohio Wesleyan's regulations into line with local and state law? Great. But what about all those years when high beer was served to minors, prospective students, local Delaware children and persons barely sober enough to hold a cup? What is the fear for years of negligence in enforcing the now important state law? Next time, call the liquor agent.

I've made an investment in this University and I'm tired of seeing its worth piddled away by people in power who hide their inability to take action with periodic flurries of policies, rules and sanctions.

When someone is found guilty of assault, throw him out. For good. When someone is found guilty of costly property damage, get rid of him. First offense, last offense. Don't foster irresponsibility with an arena of probations. Let respect for personal and property rights be taught elsewhere. The cost of teaching it here is too high.
